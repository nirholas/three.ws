360 video → splat Rolling out

Walk around, get a splat

Film a 30 second to 3 minute loop through a room, a street or a garden. Get back a photoreal Gaussian splat you can fly through, plus an MP4 flythrough to share.

  1. Upload the raw .insv or a stitched 360 MP4 in the 360 mode of Scene Capture.
  2. Each frame is split into overlapping views and the camera path is solved.
  3. A splat is trained on a GPU and opens in the splat viewer, ready to download.

360 photo → depth scene Rolling out

Step inside one photo

One 360 photo becomes a metric depth shell you can stand inside and look around, exported as a GLB mesh and a PLY point cloud.

  1. Upload a raw .insp or a stitched 360 JPEG.
  2. Depth is estimated across the whole sphere, in metres.
  3. The shell opens around you, with seams closed at the wrap.

360 photo → lighting Rolling out

Your 360 photo as your avatar's world

Use your own panorama as the backdrop and the light source for any avatar or model, so it sits in the place you shot, lit the way that place was lit.

  1. Open a model in the viewer or the splat viewer.
  2. Pick your 360 photo in the environment control.
  3. Reflections and lighting come from your panorama.

360 video → animation Rolling out

360 motion capture

A 360 camera films you from every side at once, so you never leave the frame. Turn the clip into an animation file for any rigged avatar.

  1. Upload a stitched 360 clip in the 360 mode of Video to Motion.
  2. You are tracked across the sphere and reframed into a normal view.
  3. Download GLB, BVH or FBX, or play it on your own avatar.

A place, not just a file

Open a scene in the splat viewer, light an avatar with it, or build it into a world on three.ws.

Insta360's own app builds scenes on the phone with its Spatial Capture feature. three.ws is a second path for the same footage: it runs in the browser, hands you the files, and accepts footage from any 360 camera.

Shoot so it works

Reconstruction is only as good as the footage. These nine habits make the difference between a clean scene and a smeared one.

  1. Walk slowlyA slow, even pace keeps neighbouring frames overlapping and sharp.
  2. Camera above your headOn a stick above head height you take up less of the frame and the view clears furniture.
  3. Loop back to the startFinish where you began so the path closes and the scene lines up end to end.
  4. 30 seconds to 3 minutesShorter sees too little; longer takes more time without adding much.
  5. No mirrors, glass or crowdsReflections, see-through surfaces and moving people confuse the solve.
  6. Soft, even lightOvercast daylight or diffuse indoor light beats hard sun and deep shadow.
  7. Lock exposureLock it in the camera app so brightness does not shift as you turn.
  8. Highest resolutionMore pixels per view means a sharper scene. Record at the top setting.
  9. Keep the raw .insvThe original file carries the lens calibration and motion data. Upload it, not a re-encode.
