What is three.ws/play?

three.ws/play is Coin Communities: a persistent, multiplayer 3D world that runs entirely in the browser. Think of it as an isometric MMO where the "servers" are token communities. Every world is a real place: it has terrain, buildings, NPCs, a day-night cycle, an economy, and most importantly, the other people who are there with you right now.

Three properties make it different from every "metaverse meeting" product you have seen:

  • Zero friction. It is a URL. There is no client to download, no launcher, no account wall, no GPU requirement beyond a normal laptop. If you can open a web page, you can attend.
  • Every world is a shareable link. A community's world is three.ws/play?coin=<mint>. Post that link anywhere (X, Telegram, Discord, a blog) and anyone who clicks it lands directly in that community's world, standing next to whoever else clicked it.
  • The worlds are persistent and owned by their communities. What a community builds in its world (literally, block by block, see the building section below) is saved server-side and is still there tomorrow, next week, and for the next person who drops in.

Every community can host meetups here. Yes, every one.

This is the part most people miss on the first pass, so it gets its own section.

Every Solana token already has a world on three.ws/play. Not "can request one". Not "can apply". Has one, right now, on demand. The world is derived from the coin's mint address, so the moment a community wants to gather in 3D, its venue already exists:

https://three.ws/play?coin=<your coin's mint address>

That single URL is a complete, working meetup venue for any Solana token community, of any size, at any stage. A coin that launched five minutes ago has one. Here is what hosting a meetup for your community looks like, start to finish:

  1. Get your link. Take your coin's mint address and put it in the URL above. Optionally add &name=, &symbol= and &image= so the entry card renders your branding even before the feed catches up.
  2. Post it. Drop the link in your Telegram, your X community, your Discord. On platforms that resolve oEmbed (WordPress, Ghost, Discord, dev.to, Notion), the link unfurls as an interactive preview of the world, because three.ws ships an oEmbed provider for world links.
  3. Show up. Everyone who clicks lands in the same world, in the same room, as avatars. Proximity voice, text chat, and everything else described below works immediately.
  4. Optionally, gate it. If you are the coin's creator, you can token-gate your world's Holders tier: require attendees to hold at least X of your coin to enter. The threshold is yours to set, verification happens server-side against real on-chain balances, and the general tier stays open for everyone else. Meetups for holders, town square for the curious.

No booking, no whitelist, no contact form, no fee. The venue layer of the internet's token communities is already deployed, and it is a URL scheme.

Active worlds surface automatically: the /play lobby lists the most active communities first, refreshed live from real market and community data, with the $THREE home town pinned at the top as the platform's flagship world. That pinned world is where this meetup happens.

What is actually in a world?

These are not empty rooms with floating nametags. Every world ships with the full feature set:

Avatars that are actually yours

Your three.ws avatar follows you in. Generate one from a text prompt ("a knight in obsidian armor"), from a selfie, or bring your own model: the platform rigs humanoid GLBs automatically and supports VRM avatars, with bone-name mapping for Mixamo, Avaturn, VRM/VRoid, Daz, MakeHuman and more, so any humanoid rig walks, runs and emotes with the full animation library. Guests who create nothing still get a real avatar, instantly, and you can swap avatars from inside the world.

Spatial voice chat

Walk up to someone and you hear them. Their voice comes from the direction they are standing and fades with distance, so groups form naturally, exactly like a hallway at a conference: three conversations can happen twenty meters apart without talking over each other. Voice is peer-to-peer WebRTC; the game server only relays the handshake and never carries audio.

Collaborative building

Every world has a Minecraft-style voxel layer. Players place and break blocks on a shared grid, the server validates and persists every edit per coin, and the result is there for everyone, forever. Communities decorate their own towns. For a meetup, that means your community can literally build the stage before the event.

Live events and souvenirs

A world can host a scheduled event: a countdown visible from anywhere in the plaza, an agenda that knows what is on now and what is next, a banner as each segment starts, and a synchronized fireworks show. Attendees can be granted a commemorative wearable during the window, kept forever, never sold, and never granted again after the event ends. Everything is driven by one public config file, served at three.ws/event.json.

A real in-game economy

The world runs a working economy with two deliberately separated currencies: Cash, a server-side game resource earned by gathering, fishing, combat and selling to vendor NPCs (and never cashable out), and $THREE, the platform's coin on Solana, which buys premium cosmetics and never comes from gameplay. You cannot farm the game for money and you cannot buy your way past the gameplay. There are general stores, a bank, a jobs board with daily-rotating quests, crew heists, mounts and vehicles, cosmetics with a wardrobe system, and a spin wheel. The full economy reference, rendered live from the running server's own tables, is at three.ws/play/economy.

Live market data, inside the world

Coin worlds are dressed with their coin's live data: in-world chart screens, an intel kiosk, and ambient systems that react to market activity. The world is not a themed skin; it is connected to the thing the community actually cares about.

The details that make it feel alive

Day-night cycles, distinct biomes per world (the $THREE home town has a fixed signature look: Dust Gulch, an old-west frontier town), NPCs, physics, water, a drivable car, friends lists, photo mode, and an ambient crowd so a quiet world never feels dead.

How it works: the technology

For the engineers in the room (this is an engineering community, after all), here is the actual architecture. None of this is hand-waving; all of it is running in production today.

Client

  • Three.js + WebGL, vanilla JavaScript modules, built with Vite. No framework tax, no multi-megabyte runtime.
  • Rapier physics compiled to WebAssembly for movement and collisions, loaded off the critical path so the world paints before the physics engine arrives.
  • Instanced rendering for the voxel layer: one instanced mesh per block type, so a community build of thousands of blocks stays a handful of draw calls.
  • Meshopt-compressed GLB avatars with automatic skeleton canonicalization and animation retargeting, so any humanoid rig animates correctly.

Multiplayer server

  • An authoritative Colyseus server (Node.js) running on Google Cloud Run. The browser client connects over a WebSocket.
  • One room definition, filtered by coin and tier. Every player passes their world's mint address on join, and the matchmaker only groups players who entered the same coin's community (and the same access tier). That is how every coin gets its own isolated world on the same infrastructure with zero provisioning.
  • 100 players per room instance, so a full meetup crowd stands in one room. Past the cap, the matchmaker spins up another instance of the same world automatically. The cap is a per-deploy setting, clamped server-side.
  • State sync at 15 Hz with binary delta encoding. Each player is a small schema; only fields that changed since the last patch go over the wire, roughly 28 bytes per moving player per update. A completely full 100-player room costs each client about 40 KB/s down. This is why it runs fine on a phone connection.
  • Server-side validation on every message. Movement is clamped to a physical max step (teleport hacks are rejected), positions are bounded to the world, message rates are limited per client and per message type, and every field is type-checked against an allow-list. The client is never trusted.
  • Per-coin persistence. World edits (the voxel builds) and player profiles are persisted server-side, keyed by mint, so worlds survive restarts and redeploys.
  • Event grants are server-side. The game server reads the same event config the page does, and grants a souvenir once per account, only inside the window, only in the event's world.

Voice

  • Proximity-gated WebRTC mesh. A peer connection opens only when another player comes within earshot and tears down (with hysteresis) when they leave, so the mesh stays small no matter how full the room is.
  • True spatial audio. Each remote voice runs through a Web Audio PannerNode positioned at that player's world coordinates, with the listener pinned to your avatar and oriented by your camera. Distance attenuation is linear to silence.
  • The Colyseus room relays only the SDP/ICE signaling. Audio never touches the game server.

Identity and gating

  • Guests are first-class. A persistent guest identity is minted locally, so you can attend with zero sign-up.
  • Wallet sign-in uses a signed nonce challenge; the verified wallet becomes your account across every world, and a session lasts 30 days.
  • Holder passes are minted server-side. To enter a gated Holders world, the server reads your linked wallet's actual on-chain balance of that exact mint, prices it, and signs a short-lived pass the game server verifies on join. The browser never asserts what you hold, so passes cannot be forged for someone else's address.
  • Creator-controlled gates. A coin's on-chain creator (verified server-side against the launch record, never self-asserted) can set the holding threshold for their world's Holders tier, or clear it back to the platform default.

The API surface

Everything the /play client uses is a real, public HTTP API you can build on. The interesting ones for community builders:

EndpointWhat it does
GET /api/community/worldsThe live world lobby as JSON: the most active coin communities, each with token, symbol, name, art and activity stats. This is the same feed the /play lobby renders.
GET /api/community/world-gate?token=<mint>Read a world's entry gate: whether the Holders tier is token-gated and at what threshold. POST (creator only, verified server-side) sets it.
POST /api/community/holder-pass?token=<mint>Mints a signed holder pass for the authenticated user after checking real on-chain balances. This is how gated entry works.
GET /event.jsonThe live event config: name, window, agenda and souvenir. The /event page, the lobby banner, the in-world countdown and the server-side souvenir grant all read this one file, so they can never disagree about when the event is.
GET /api/play-oembed?url=<world url>oEmbed provider: makes a world link unfurl as an interactive embed on WordPress, Ghost, Discord, dev.to, Notion and any embed.ly/iframely-backed editor.
GET /api/coin/trending, /api/coin/<mint>/..., /api/coin/ohlc and friendsThe live market data layer the worlds are dressed with: trending, detail, OHLC candles, markets, news, fear/greed and more.
GET /play/economyNot an API, but the live, self-documenting economy reference: every price, vendor table and gate, rendered from the running server's own data.

Create: free AI 3D generation, no account required

three.ws is a free AI 3D model generator: describe anything in plain language at three.ws/create and get a downloadable, textured GLB in about a minute. No key, no account, no 3D software. The same pipeline covers image to 3D (a photo or sketch becomes a model), selfie to 3D avatar (one photo becomes a rigged, animation-ready character), and prompt to avatar. Around it sits a full production suite: Forge Max and Forge Studio for higher-fidelity generation, Restyle Studio for PBR materials and AI restyling, Scene Studio and Compose for scene assembly, Diorama for text-to-world generation, AR Studio for viewing creations in your actual room, Mocap Studio and Video to Motion for turning video into animation, Capture and Splat for photo-based reconstruction, Voice and Lipsync for talking avatars, and Daily Forge, a daily creation challenge with streaks. Every generated and purchased model ships with a signed provenance credential.

Animate: universal rigging, no rig allowlist

Any humanoid model animates on three.ws. The platform auto-rigs generated characters, and a skeleton canonicalizer maps Mixamo, Avaturn, VRM/VRoid, Daz, MakeHuman, Unreal and other bone conventions onto one canonical skeleton, so the shared library of 2,800+ animations (animations, gestures, choreograph) retargets onto anything humanoid, legs included. VRM avatars keep their spring bones and expressions. The Rig Doctor tells you in seconds whether a model will animate and why. There is a full character library, a pose studio, a wardrobe system, and sign-language animation support.

Embed and distribute: your agent, everywhere

Everything you create is built to leave the platform. The <agent-3d> web component embeds a live, animated, embeddable 3D agent in any website with one script tag, with animations, moods and speech drivable from page code, and one-paste embeds for WordPress, Framer and Webflow. A family of npm products extends it: a page-walking companion (walk), a talking page guide, a guided-tour engine, an embeddable concierge, and an assistant widget. A three.ws avatar can be the face of a LiveKit Agents or Pipecat voice pipeline, lipsyncing in real time. World and creation links unfurl as interactive 3D embeds via oEmbed on WordPress, Ghost, Discord, dev.to and Notion, and a Discord Activity brings a shared Forge room into any voice channel. Mobile gets AR-ready "view in your space" exports. The Embed Doctor debugs any embed in place.

The agent economy: x402 agent payments on Solana

three.ws runs a production x402 pay-per-call economy: APIs charge per request in a dollar stablecoin, settled on-chain on Solana. An agent hits a 402 Payment Required, pays, retries, and gets its answer, with no API keys and no subscriptions. Live today: a paid market-data API, a fabric of 1,000,000+ individually priced data endpoints, a unified service catalog at x402 with a studio for making your own endpoints payable, buyer-side purchase receipts, on-chain agent identities, agent reputation, agent wallets with owner-signed spend caps, a fleet monitor, a live wall of agent activity, an agent exchange, agent-to-agent commerce with invoices and signed fulfillment records, and a labor market where agents hire agents. The platform's coin is $THREE on Solana; coins launched through the platform appear in the launch directory.

Market data and crypto tools

A full live market-data surface backs the worlds and the agents: the crypto hub, prices, a heatmap, a screener, fear and greed, a converter, DeFi, stablecoins, market news, coin intel, and a free crypto data API (crypto-api) alongside the free 3D API.

For developers

The developer platform is the connective tissue: docs with a quick start and start here path, runnable samples, a cookbook, an examples gallery, and a complete REST API for agents with signed webhooks. Around 40 MCP servers are published under the @three-ws scope on npm, with the core servers listed on the official Anthropic MCP Registry: 3D generation, avatars, scenes, spatial responses, market intel, payments and more, usable from Claude, Cursor, or any MCP-capable client. Two are IBM-specific and belong in this community's toolbox: @three-ws/ibm-watsonx-mcp (watsonx.ai chat, generation, embeddings, tokenization and forecasting, running on your own IBM Cloud account and credentials) and @three-ws/ibm-x402-mcp (IBM Granite tools exposed over MCP with per-call x402 billing). Both are community-built and open source; IBM does not operate them. 90+ npm packages ship overall: the avatar SDK and CLI, GLB tools, animation retargeting, mocap, voice, sign-language recognition, the Solana agent SDK, and payment SDKs. Machine-readable surfaces cover the rest: openapi.json, llms.txt, sitemap.xml, and .well-known/mcp.json.

Where this is going

Directional, not dated. At the first meetup this list said "next: event tooling". That shipped, and it is what runs this hour: the countdown, the in-world agenda, the fireworks and the souvenir. Here is the shape of the work from here:

  • Now: making a first visit effortless on every device. A phone gets a world sized for it, onboarding gets a first-time visitor walking and talking in under a minute, and the world tells you who is actually there before you walk in.
  • Next: events any community can run without us. The same event layer this meetup uses, opened up so a community schedules its own session, its own agenda and its own commemorative wearable in its own world, plus richer creator controls over world dressing and gates.
  • Then: scale-out. The room architecture already shards horizontally; cluster-aware matchmaking across machines is a config-level change on the current stack, planned for when sustained concurrency demands it. The goal is boring: any community, any size, any time, one URL.

If you want to influence this list, that is what the meetup's open Q&A is for. Come tell the three.ws team what your community needs to run its events here.

FAQ

Do I need a crypto wallet to attend?
No. The $THREE home town's general tier is open, and guests get a full avatar, full voice and chat, and the event souvenir. A wallet only matters for holder-gated tiers and for buying cosmetics.

Do I need to install anything?
No. It is a web page. Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari on a normal laptop all work, and phones get a lighter world built for them. Voice asks for microphone permission like any web call.

I have never used a 3D world. Will I be lost?
You will be walking within 30 seconds: click to move or use WASD, walk up to people to hear them. Join 15 minutes early and we will help you personally.

How do I get the souvenir?
Be in the $THREE home town at any point between 8:00 and 9:00 AM PT. The Meetup Orbit Crown is granted automatically: a card slides in with a Wear it button, and the crown waits in My Fits if you dismiss the card. Sign in with a wallet if you want it to follow you across devices; guests keep it on the browser they attended from.

I came to an earlier meetup. Is this the same session?
Same venue, new content. The world now runs the agenda itself, Video to Motion and the souvenir are new since August, and the showcase is whatever you bring.

Can my community really use this for our own events, today?
Yes. Your world already exists at three.ws/play?coin=<your mint>. Post the link and you have a venue. Nothing to request, nothing to pay.

Is this financial advice / do I need to buy anything?
No and no. The event is free and the platform is free to use. $THREE is the platform's coin and gates some cosmetic extras; attendance requires holding nothing.
