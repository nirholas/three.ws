The Three.ws User Group Meets Inside the World Again, With IBM as Sponsor

By Jessica Swanson. The event listing is on this group's Events tab; the full venue guide is in the meetup discussion thread pinned on the group home page.

In August this group did something most developer communities have not tried: it held a meetup somewhere other than a video call. We gathered inside three.ws itself, as avatars in a browser-based 3D world, and the questions afterward were the same two every time: when do we meet in the world again, and can I bring my own demo? The group has met once since, online, for a live build of an embedded 3D agent in thirty minutes. This Friday answers both questions: we are back in the world, and the showcase segment is yours.

The Three.ws User Group's third meetup, and its second inside the world, is on Friday, November 6, and IBM Community is sponsoring it. It is still free, still in the browser, and still in the flagship world at three.ws/play. What is different is the agenda: almost every segment is something the platform did not have the last time we met in the world.

One note on the clock: the US moves off daylight saving time on November 1, so for anyone in Europe or Asia this session lands an hour later on your local clock than the August meetup did. The table above already accounts for it.

How to join

  1. Open three.ws/play at the event time and click the pinned world at the top of the list, or open three.ws/event and use the button there.
  2. Pick an avatar. Guests get one automatically, but the better move is to build your own 3D AI agent beforehand at three.ws/create from a text prompt or a selfie; it follows you into the world.
  3. Walk up to people and talk. Voice chat is spatial: you hear the people near you, from the direction they stand, and they fade as you walk away. Text chat works too.

New to browser-based 3D worlds? Arrive at 7:45 AM Pacific. The three.ws team will be at the spawn point to get you moving before we start.

What is on the agenda

The world runs the schedule itself this time. A countdown appears in the home town the day before, and each segment announces itself in the world as it starts.

  • Doors open (8:00). A welcome from IBM Community and the three.ws team.
  • One sentence to a rigged 3D agent (8:06). A text prompt becomes a rigged, animated avatar in about a minute, and then someone in the room wears it.
  • Video to motion (8:14). A short phone clip becomes an animation you can download and put on any humanoid avatar.
  • Forge into the world (8:20). Type a sentence in chat and a generated 3D object lands in the world for everyone standing there.
  • An agent pays for a tool (8:27). An AI agent hits a paywall, settles a micropayment on its own, gets its answer, and leaves a receipt anyone can open.
  • Community showcase (8:33). Members show what they have built. If you have an avatar, an embed, an MCP integration or an agent, bring it.
  • Open Q&A and what ships next (8:43). Architecture, roadmap, how specific features work.
  • Group photo (8:55). We gather at the totem for a photo, and fireworks close the hour.

Everyone who is in the world during the hour also receives a commemorative wearable, the Meetup Orbit Crown, that is granted only during this session and never again. It is a small thing, and it is the kind of small thing people keep.

Why this format keeps earning the hour

The venue is the demo. three.ws/play is a persistent multiplayer 3D world that runs entirely in the browser, with spatial voice, text chat, collaborative building and live data on in-world screens. An hour at the meetup doubles as an hour of hands-on evaluation of the technology this group exists to discuss, and nobody has to install anything to take part.

It is also a working answer to a question a lot of us in developer communities keep asking: what comes after the webinar? Every world on the platform is a plain shareable URL, so a community that wants to try this format for its own events does not have to request anything. What you see on Friday is what any community can run next week.

If you want the deep technical background before you arrive (the multiplayer architecture, the rigging system, the agent payment rail, and the full list of what has shipped since August), the meetup discussion thread pinned on the group home page carries the full write-up.
