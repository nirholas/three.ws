Join the Three.ws User Group's Second In-World Meetup

When this user group launched in July, the promise was more than threads and blog posts: it was live sessions with the people actually building AI agents with 3D capabilities. In August we held the first one inside the platform itself, then met online for a live build of an embedded 3D agent in thirty minutes. On Friday, November 6, we go back into the world, with IBM Community sponsoring the session, and I want to make sure nobody misses it because they assumed it was another webinar link.

It is not a video call. The three.ws team is hosting the group's third meetup, and its second in-world one, inside the platform itself, in the flagship world at three.ws/play. You attend as an avatar, you hear the people standing near you through spatial voice chat, and the demos happen in the world around you rather than on slides.

If you missed August: the meetup was billed as an hour, the world stayed open all day, and people stayed with it for nine hours. Anyone with the link could walk in, and the room peaked at 3,145 avatars at once. The team's account of the day is at three.ws/blog/ibm-user-group-first-in-world-meetup-recap.

How to join

  1. Open three.ws/play at the event time and click the pinned world at the top of the list, or open three.ws/event and use the button there.
  2. Pick an avatar. Guests get one automatically, but the better move is to build your own custom 3D AI agent beforehand at three.ws/create from a text prompt or a selfie; it follows you into the world.
  3. Walk up to people and talk. Voice chat is spatial: you hear the people near you, from the direction they stand, and they fade as you walk away. Text chat works too.

If you have never been in a browser-based 3D world before, arrive fifteen minutes early. The three.ws team will be at the spawn point from 7:45 AM Pacific, and you will be walking and talking before the event starts.

What is on the agenda

The world runs the schedule itself this time: a countdown appears in the home town the day before, and each segment announces itself in the world as it starts.

  • Doors open (8:00). A welcome from IBM Community and the three.ws team.
  • One sentence to a rigged 3D agent (8:06). A text prompt becomes a rigged, animated avatar in about a minute, and then someone in the room wears it.
  • Video to motion (8:14). A short phone clip becomes an animation you can download and put on any humanoid avatar.
  • Forge into the world (8:20). Type a sentence in chat and a generated 3D object lands in the world for everyone standing there.
  • An agent pays for a tool (8:27). An AI agent hits a paywall, settles a micropayment on its own, gets its answer, and leaves a receipt anyone can open.
  • Community demos (8:33). If you have built something with the platform (an avatar, an embed, an MCP integration, an agent), bring it. There is time set aside for members to show their work.
  • Open Q&A and what ships next (8:43). Architecture, roadmap, how specific features work. The team has said the skeptical questions are the most useful ones, so hold nothing back.
  • Group photo (8:55). We gather at the totem for a photo, and fireworks close the hour.

Everyone who is in the world during the hour also receives a commemorative wearable, the Meetup Orbit Crown, that is granted only during this session and never again.

Why I think this format is worth your hour

The venue is the demo. three.ws/play is a persistent multiplayer 3D world that runs entirely in the browser: spatial voice, text chat, a collaborative building layer, a working in-game economy, and live data on in-world screens. An hour spent attending the meetup doubles as an hour of hands-on evaluation of the technology this group exists to discuss, and every world there is a plain shareable URL, so what you learn on Friday applies directly to running events of your own.

If you want the deep technical background before you arrive (the text-to-3D pipeline, the universal rigging system, the multiplayer architecture, the x402 agent-payment rail, and everything that has shipped since August), the event listing and the meetup thread pinned on the group home page carry the full write-up.
